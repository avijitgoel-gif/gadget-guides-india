## The number on the box is not your speed

Every adapter here plugs into USB 2.0. TP-Link's own spec sheets list "Interface: USB 2.0" for the T2U Nano, the TX10UB Nano, the TL-WN823N and the TL-WN725N, and the T2U Plus listing says USB 2.0 too. USB-IF, the body that runs the USB standard, rates USB 2.0 at 480Mbps.

So a "900Mbps" Wi-Fi 6 dongle on USB 2.0 can never move data faster than 480Mbps, and real transfers are lower than that once overhead is taken out. TP-Link says so in its fine print: the maximum rates are "physical rates derived from IEEE Standard 802.11 specifications" and actual speed "may be limited by the rate of the product's USB port".

There is a second catch. The headline figure adds both bands together. The Archer T2U Nano is sold as AC600, but TP-Link lists it at up to 433Mbps on 5GHz and 200Mbps on 2.4GHz. Your PC connects on one band at a time, so the most you get on 5GHz is 433Mbps as a link rate.

In practice, on a 100Mbps or 200Mbps broadband plan, a dual band AC600 adapter on 5GHz is usually enough. Paying more for Wi-Fi 6 on USB 2.0 gets you the Bluetooth radio and Wi-Fi 6 features, not a way past the USB limit.

## Buying guide: four checks before you buy

### Check whether your router has 5GHz

Open the Wi-Fi list on your phone. If you see two network names, often one ending in "5G", your router is dual band. Buy a dual band adapter (T2U Nano, T2U Plus, TX10UB Nano or BlueFi Nano) to use the 5GHz band. If you only see one name, a cheaper 2.4GHz adapter is enough.

### Check your operating system

TP-Link lists the TX10UB Nano for Windows 11 and 10 only. The Portronics listing says Windows 10 and above. The older TP-Link Wi-Fi 4 and Wi-Fi 5 models list Windows XP to 11 and macOS 10.15 and earlier, and the TL-WN823N and TL-WN725N add Linux. TP-Link also warns that you may need to update the driver after an OS update, so keep its download centre page bookmarked.

### Check WPA3 if your router uses it

The TX10UB Nano lists WPA3. The T2U Nano lists only WEP and WPA/WPA2. TP-Link India's support page says the mixed WPA2-PSK/WPA3-SAE setting was introduced so that WPA2-only devices can still connect, so an older adapter should join a router in mixed mode but not one set to WPA3 only.

### Use a front or extension port for a desktop

A nano adapter at the back of a metal tower near the floor gets weaker signal. A front USB port or a short USB extension cable to bring it up onto the desk often helps more than a faster adapter.

## FAQ

### Which is the best USB WiFi adapter for PC under Rs 1,500 in India?
The TP-Link Archer T2U Nano at ₹1,123 is the pick. On 18 November 2026 it had 4.3 stars from 19,215 ratings on Amazon.in, dual band AC600 support and a 3 year warranty. If you also want Bluetooth on Windows 10 or 11, the Archer TX10UB Nano at ₹1,449 adds Wi-Fi 6 and Bluetooth 5.3.

### Is a 900Mbps Wi-Fi 6 USB adapter really 900Mbps?
No. 900Mbps is the two bands added together as maximum signal rates, and TP-Link lists the TX10UB Nano on USB 2.0, which USB-IF rates at 480Mbps. Real throughput is below both numbers.

### Will a 2.4GHz adapter connect to a 5GHz WiFi network?
No. Adapters such as the TP-Link TL-WN823N and TL-WN725N work only on 2.400 to 2.4835GHz, so they cannot see a 5GHz network. Use a dual band adapter like the Archer T2U Nano if you want 5GHz.

### Do USB WiFi adapters work on Mac?
Only some, and only older macOS versions. TP-Link lists the T2U Nano, T2U Plus, TL-WN823N and TL-WN725N for macOS 10.15 and earlier, and the TX10UB Nano for Windows 11 and 10 only.

### Do I need a driver CD for a USB WiFi adapter?
The older TP-Link models ship with a resource CD, but most PCs no longer have a CD drive, so download the driver from TP-Link's site instead. The TX10UB Nano has its driver pre-loaded on the adapter.
