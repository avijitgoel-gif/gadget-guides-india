## Why does an extender cut my speed?

TP-Link's help page puts it simply: "the more wireless hops, the slower the speed would be". A phone connected to the extender goes through one more wireless hop than a phone connected to the router, so it gets less speed.

TP-Link's business networking blog adds the reason. A basic single-radio extender uses one radio on one channel to talk to both the router and your devices, and WiFi radios cannot send and receive on the same channel at once. So each piece of data uses the channel twice, which can cut available throughput by roughly half, before walls and distance take their share.

Three things reduce the hit:

- **Use a wire if you can.** TP-Link says there is no speed difference for a device that is wired to the extender's Ethernet port.
- **Use dual band smartly.** Modes like the RE200's High Speed Mode use one band for the router link and the other for your devices.
- **Use Access Point mode.** If you can run a LAN cable from the router, the TL-WA850RE, RE200 and RE305 listings all mention an access point mode, which skips the wireless hop entirely.

## Where to place a WiFi extender

TP-Link's guidance lists three placement mistakes:

1. **Too close to the router:** it adds very little new coverage.
2. **Inside the dead zone:** it picks up a weak signal and rebroadcasts a weak signal.
3. **Behind the same wall or floor that blocks the router:** it inherits the same problem.

The sweet spot is about halfway between the router and the dead zone, where your phone still shows a decent signal from the router. The RE205 and RE305 listings mention a signal light that shows when you have found a good spot, and the Tether app can help with the others.

## Extender, mesh or a second router?

TP-Link's own blog says an extender works best for one or two specific weak spots, and that whole-home WiFi problems usually call for a mesh system instead. If you need coverage across three floors, ₹3,000 is better spent towards a mesh kit than on two extenders. For one dead bedroom, a balcony or a terrace camera, a single extender is the cheaper fix.

### Which is the best WiFi range extender under ₹3,000 in India?
The TP-Link RE305 at ₹2,849 is the strongest pick under ₹3,000, with AC1200 dual band (867 Mbps on 5 GHz), OneMesh support, a 3 year warranty and 4.3 stars from 8,822 ratings on 13 November 2026. The RE200 at ₹2,199 is the value dual band pick.

### Will a WiFi extender increase my internet speed?
No. TP-Link says an extender only extends your existing WiFi and cannot make your connection faster than your plan. Devices connected through it usually get less speed than devices next to the router, because of the extra wireless hop.

### Single band or dual band extender?
A single band 2.4 GHz extender like the TL-WA850RE is enough for smart plugs, bulbs, cameras and browsing. If you stream or work on video calls in the far room, pick a dual band model like the RE200 or RE305.

### Does a TP-Link extender work with Jio Fiber or Airtel routers?
TP-Link's listings say these extenders work with any WiFi router or access point. OneMesh on the RE305 only works with a TP-Link OneMesh router, and some RE205 buyers on Amazon reported trouble with the Jio Fibre 5 GHz band.

### Where should I place my WiFi extender?
About halfway between the router and the dead zone, where it still gets a good signal from the router. Do not put it inside the dead zone or behind the same wall that blocks the router.
