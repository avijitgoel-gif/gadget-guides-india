## What "STQC Certified" in a listing title really means

### The rule in one paragraph

CCTV cameras have long needed BIS registration under MeitY's Compulsory Registration Order for safety (IS 13252 Part 1). MeitY then notified security Essential Requirements, and BIS set 9 April 2025 as the last date. The requirements cover things like secure boot, secure firmware updates, protection of keys and access control. In March 2025 MeitY clarified that analog CCTV cameras are exempt, so the rule bites on network cameras, which includes every Wi-Fi camera in this guide.

### STQC's list stopped, the BIS register did not

Many titles say "STQC Certified". STQC did run the original testing scheme, but its own website now says the certified product list for network cameras "is no longer being updated and is retained solely for historical reference", following a MeitY circular dated 4 February 2026. New registrations now show up in the BIS Compulsory Registration register with an R-number.

In practice, "STQC Certified" in a title is a marketing phrase. The thing to check is the BIS R-number for your exact model.

### The registrant is often not the brand

In the public register data we checked, the CP Plus Wi-Fi cameras in this guide (CP-E25Q, CP-E31Q, CP-E35Q) and Qubo's camera models are registered by AIL Dixon Technologies, a manufacturing company, not by CP Plus or Qubo directly. The MANOMAY M1078S, Trueview T18296S and Godrej's registered models are all registered by Warner Electronics India. That is normal for contract manufacturing. It just means you should search the register by model number, not by brand name.

### How to check before you buy

1. Find the model code on the listing (for example CP-E35Q, M1078S or T18296S).
2. Search that code on the BIS registration portal (crsbis.in) or a register tool.
3. Confirm the registration is still valid. The CP Plus entries above show validity to 20 May 2027; the MANOMAY entry to 7 October 2027.
4. If the listing shows no model code at all, ask the seller for it. The Qubo listings we checked show "Camera" or "1" in the model number field, and the Godrej EVE Pro 3 listing shows an item code (46171610SD01412) that does not match any Godrej model code in the register data. That does not prove they are unregistered. It means the listing does not let you check.

## Listing contradictions we found

### Qubo: "No SD Card Required" in the title, a microSD slot in the box

Both Qubo 3MP listings say "No SD Card Required, Records Directly on Cloud" in the title. Qubo's own product page lists "Compatible Storage: Cloud or microSD (upto 1TB)". Qubo's support page for the same camera says it supports "up to 128 GB". Qubo's store page explains the title: cloud-only recording "needs an active AI Guard plan" and "applies to models manufactured post May 2026".

So "no SD card required" really means "no SD card required if you pay for cloud". Qubo's AI Guard page lists three premium plans with 7, 14 and 28 days of cloud backup, priced from ₹149 (and ₹1,490 on the longer billing option) up to ₹399 and ₹3,990. Meanwhile one Amazon bullet says plans start "from Rs. 99/month". Check the app for the current price before you count on cloud. If you do not want a subscription, budget for a microSD card and keep it at 128GB to stay inside the lower of Qubo's two stated limits.

There is a second inconsistency. The ₹2,745 Qubo listing (ASIN B0G64G64YL) says "Designed, built and manufactured in India" in its bullets, yet its product details table says Country of Origin: China. The ₹2,990 Qubo listing (B0H7BSCC4B) and Qubo's own site say India. If origin matters to you, the higher-priced listing is the consistent one.

### CP Plus: tilt angle and resolution typos

CP Plus's official CP-E35Q datasheet gives pan 355 degrees and tilt 59 degrees, with a maximum of 20 frames per second. The Amazon listing says "360° Pan & 85° Tilt". Trust the datasheet. 20fps is fine for home monitoring.

The CP-E25Q listing is a 2MP, 1080p camera, yet its storage bullet calls it "This CP PLUS 3MP smart Wi-Fi camera". If you want 3MP, buy the CP-E35Q or CP-E31Q.

## Things that matter more than megapixels

### Your router must have 2.4GHz

CP Plus's quick install guide says its ezyKam+ cameras "can't connect to 5GHz networks", and Qubo's spec sheet lists 2.4GHz Wi-Fi only. Several Amazon reviewers for both brands report setup trouble on dual-band routers. If your router merges 2.4GHz and 5GHz under one name, split them during setup, or use the CP-E31Q's LAN port. A 64GB card is enough for event recording on most of them; our [64GB memory card guide]({{ "/best-64gb-memory-card-for-mobile-in-india/" | relative_url }}) covers which cards hold up. Qubo says its camera stores clips at 480p by default and that about 8 hours of recording takes 1GB. Remember that a card can be stolen with the camera, which is the real case for cloud backup.
