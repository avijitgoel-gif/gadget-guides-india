## Qi, Qi2 and "Qi2 enabled": what the label really means

### Plain Qi pads: often 7.5W on an iPhone

Apple's support page says any iPhone 8 or later works with Qi certified chargers, and that many of them charge an iPhone "at rates up to 7.5 watts". Apple's charge speed page adds that a wireless charger that provides less than 10W, "like a Qi1-certified charger", can make charging take longer than you expect.

So a ₹499 pad that says 15W can still be a 7.5W charger for an iPhone.

### Qi2 is the magnetic version

The WPC says Qi2 devices use magnetic attachment to line up the phone and charger, which improves efficiency and charging speed. Qi2 gives 15W. A newer tier, Qi2 25W, gives up to 25W.

On the iPhone side, iOS 17.2 added Qi2 support for iPhone 13 and 14 models, as 9to5Mac reported from Apple's release notes, and The Verge reported that iOS 17.4 quietly added it to the iPhone 12. The iPhone 15 shipped with it. That makes every MagSafe iPhone from the 12 onward able to charge at 15W on a certified Qi2 pad.

### "Qi2 enabled" or "Qi2 compatible" is not the same thing

This is the trap in this price band. The WPC's own guide to Qi2 chargers says products that are certified will say "Qi2 certified" and will be in its database, and that "Qi2 compatible" or "Qi2 enabled" means the product is not certified.

Portronics sells two almost identical magnetic chargers on Amazon.in. The Flux 2 (POR-2135) says "Qi2 Certified" in its title and matches a WPC record. The older Flux (POR-2132, ₹1,329) says "Qi2 Enabled" and we found no WPC record for it. The Flux had 2.9 stars from 48 ratings when we checked. Spend the extra ₹120.

### Samsung phones: check for WPC certification

Samsung says Galaxy A series, XCover and Tab devices do not support wireless charging at all. If you own a Galaxy A phone, none of these chargers will work for it.

Galaxy S and Z phones do support Fast Wireless Charging, including the Galaxy S24 and S25 series per Samsung UK's charger table. Samsung UK also says fast wireless charging is only supported on chargers certified by Samsung and the WPC, and may be limited on chargers without that certification. So a cheap uncertified pad will usually charge a Galaxy, just not at its fastest.

## How to check any charger in 30 seconds

1. Scroll to the "Technical Details" table on the Amazon page and copy the **Model Number** or **Manufacturer Part Number**.
2. Open the [WPC Qi certified product database](https://jpsapi.wirelesspowerconsortium.com/products/qi) and search for the brand.
3. Look for that part number. For a Qi2 charger, the Power Profile column should say **MPP** and Load Power **15**. For Qi2 25W it should say **MPP25** and **25**.
4. No match? Treat the charger as a basic pad, whatever the title says.

When we did this on 24 November 2026, the database listed a Portronics "Wireless Charge Stand" with part number FLUX 2 (MPP, 15W, certified 10 March 2025). It also listed a Mozen by Amkette "Mozen Air Wireless Charger" (MPP, 15W, certified 28 January 2026), but the Amazon page for the Air 450 gives the part number "PowerPro Air 450", so we could not confirm it is the same record. We found no record for the UNIGEN UP200CCPK, Spigen PF2104 or Portronics POR-2123.

### Will a 15W wireless charger charge my iPhone at 15W?
Only if it is Qi2 certified or an Apple MagSafe charger. Apple says many Qi certified chargers charge iPhone at up to 7.5W, and recommends a MagSafe or Qi2 certified charger for faster wireless charging.

### What is the difference between Qi2 certified and Qi2 enabled?
A Qi2 certified charger has passed WPC testing and appears in the WPC certified product database. The WPC says terms like "Qi2 compatible" or "Qi2 enabled" mean the product is not certified.

### Does my Samsung Galaxy A phone support wireless charging?

### Do these wireless chargers come with a power adapter?
