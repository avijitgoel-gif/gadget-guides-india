## How to choose a Bluetooth speaker under ₹2,000

### Watts on the box are not all equal

Some brands quote RMS output (continuous power), others quote a peak or "maximum" figure. The boAt Stone 352 says 10W RMS. Most others just say a wattage. Use the number to compare speakers from the same brand, and lean on rating counts and reviews to judge real loudness across brands.

### Read the battery claim carefully

Battery claims are measured at a set volume. Mivi quotes 24 hours at 70% volume, boAt quotes 12 hours at 60% for the Stone 352, and ZEBRONICS quotes 8 hours at 50% with LEDs off. RGB lights, high volume and bass boost all cut playtime. If you charge your speaker from a power bank on trips, our [power bank under ₹1,500 guide]({{ "/best-power-bank-under-1500-in-india/" | relative_url }}) covers options that also top up a phone.

### Water rating

IPX7 (Mivi Roam 2, boAt Stone 352, JBL Go Essential) means the speaker is rated to survive immersion in water. IPX5 and IPX4 cover splashes and sweat. "Splash resistant" or "waterproof" with no IPX grade is a marketing line, so treat it as no rating.

### Warranty and invoice

Big brands here give 1 year. Dubstep and HAMMER give 6 months. Buy from a seller that issues a GST invoice, and keep it, because service centres usually ask for it. Speakers sold as "JBL compatible" or "jbl mini" by unknown sellers at under ₹300 are not JBL products. Check that the brand name on the listing is the actual maker.

### TWS, mic and inputs

TWS lets you pair two of the same speaker for stereo. A built-in mic lets you take calls, which the JBL Go Essential lacks. FM radio and USB or microSD playback, found on the Astra 35 and SoundDrum 1, matter if older family members want music without a phone.
