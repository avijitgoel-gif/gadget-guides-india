## How to choose a smart bulb in India

### Wi-Fi or Bluetooth

This is the single most important choice. A Wi-Fi bulb connects to your home router, so you can control it from anywhere, use Alexa or Google Assistant, and run schedules. A Bluetooth-only bulb, like the Wipro NS9600, works only from a phone within range. Check the listing for the words "Bluetooth only" before you buy.

### Check that your router has 2.4 GHz

Budget smart bulbs connect on the 2.4 GHz band, not 5 GHz. The Halonix listing states this directly, and one Halonix reviewer said pairing failed on 5 GHz and worked as soon as they switched to 2.4 GHz. Most dual-band routers broadcast both. If yours combines them under one name, you may need to separate them in the router settings during setup. The same rule applies to smart plugs, which we cover in our [smart plug under ₹1,000 guide]({{ "/best-smart-plug-under-1000-in-india/" | relative_url }}).

### Lumens matter more than watts

Wattage tells you how much power the bulb draws, not how bright it is. The Tapo uses 8.7W for 806 lumens, the Halonix uses 12W for 810 lumens, and the Qubo and Wipro 12.5W bulbs list 1,200 lumens. For a main room light, aim for the 1,200 lumen bulbs. Several reviews across brands also note that coloured modes are much dimmer than white, so do not expect a red or blue setting to light a room.

### Why smart bulbs drop off Wi-Fi

Connectivity complaints appear in the review summaries of every bulb in this list. The most common causes buyers describe are power cuts, a weak signal at the far end of the house, and switching the bulb off at the wall, which disconnects it. A smart bulb needs power at the holder all the time to stay online, so leave the wall switch on and control it from the app or by voice. If your area has frequent voltage swings, our sister site's [voltage stabilizer guide](https://avijitgoel-gif.github.io/best-voltage-stabilizer-for-tv-under-2500-in-india/) explains when protection helps.

### What it costs to run

At 9W, a bulb used for 5 hours a day uses about 1.35 units (kWh) a month. At an example tariff of ₹8 per unit, that is roughly ₹11 a month. The 12.5W bulbs come to about ₹15 a month on the same assumptions. Your own tariff will differ, so check your electricity bill.

### Bulb or smart plug?

If you want to automate a table lamp, fan or heater rather than a ceiling light, a smart plug is the better buy. If you want colour and dimming, you need a smart bulb. Many people end up with both.

### Do smart bulbs need a hub in India?
No. All the Wi-Fi bulbs in this guide connect directly to your home router. The Tapo L530B listing specifically states that no hub is required.

### Do smart bulbs work on 5 GHz Wi-Fi?
Budget smart bulbs connect on 2.4 GHz Wi-Fi. The Halonix listing states this, and buyers report pairing only works once they switch their phone to the 2.4 GHz band during setup.

### Will a smart bulb work if I switch it off at the wall?
