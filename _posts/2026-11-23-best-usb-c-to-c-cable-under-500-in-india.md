## 60W, 65W, 100W: what the label really means

### A normal USB-C cable is a 3A, 60W cable

USB Power Delivery tops out at 20V on a standard cable. At 3A that is 60W. The USB-IF's own e-marker test document says every USB-C to USB-C cable needs an e-marker except one type: a USB 2.0 only cable rated at 3A. That is exactly what most cheap cables are, which is why they can skip the chip.

So the Ambrane RCTT15 and ZEBRONICS ZEB-TT60+ are honest when they say 3A and 60W. That is the full rating for a cable of this kind.

### "65W" is a marketing number, not a cable class

The USB-IF says USB-C to USB-C cables must be labelled as either 60W or 240W to pass its compliance program. UL Solutions, which tests cables, notes that USB-IF stopped certifying the older 100W (20V, 5A) class on 22 December 2021, and that cables now come only as 60W or 240W.

There is no 65W cable class. A 65W charger reaches 65W at 20V and 3.25A. Samsung's own 65W trio charger, the EP-T6530, lists exactly that: 20V at 3.25A on its main port. A 3A cable cannot carry 3.25A by the standard, so with a 3A cable the charger should fall back to 60W. The boAt listing says 65W but does not give a current rating or mention an e-marker. HAMMER, which we left out because it showed no price on 23 November, says 3.25A on its listing.

For a phone this rarely matters. Most phones charge well under 60W anyway.

### When you actually need a 5A e-marked cable

You need one in two cases.

1. **Samsung Super Fast Charging 2.0 on older Ultra and Plus models.** Samsung UK says that to use super fast charging with its 45W charger you need the 45W adapter plus a 5A e-marked cable. Samsung's Philippines support page adds that Galaxy S25 and later models get Super Fast Charging 2.0 with the 3A cable in the box, so this matters most for the S22, S23 and S24 Ultra and Plus models.
2. **Laptops that charge above 60W.** A 65W or 100W laptop charger needs a 5A cable to deliver more than 60W.

In this price band, the Portronics Konnect B is the only cable we found that states both 5A and an e-marker chip on its Amazon.in page. The Anker 643 says it supports up to 100W, which needs 5A, though its listing does not use the word e-marker.

## Data speed: all of these are 480 Mbps

Every cable here lists 480 Mbps or USB 2.0. That is fine for charging, syncing a phone and moving photos.

It is not fine if you own an iPhone 15 Pro or later Pro model. Apple says those models support USB 3 speeds up to 10 Gbit per second only "when using an optional USB 3 cable", and that the cable in the iPhone box "supports charging and USB 2 speeds". If you shoot ProRes video and offload it to a Mac, buy a cable marked 10Gbps. None of the cables in this guide qualifies, and genuine 10Gbps cables usually cost more than ₹500.

The same applies if you want to plug a phone or laptop into a monitor. For that, look at a hub or a cable that lists DisplayPort Alt Mode, like the ones in our [USB-C hub guide]({{ "/best-usb-c-hub-under-2000-in-india/" | relative_url }}).

### Is a 65W cable better than a 60W cable?
Not by itself. USB-IF recognises only 60W and 240W USB-C to USB-C cable classes. A standard 3A cable carries up to 60W. Going above 60W needs a 5A e-marked cable, so check the current rating, not the wattage in the title.

### Do I need a 5A cable for my Samsung phone?
Only for Super Fast Charging 2.0 with a 45W charger on models such as the Galaxy S22, S23 and S24 Ultra and Plus. Samsung says this needs a 5A e-marked cable. Samsung also says Galaxy S25 and later get Super Fast Charging 2.0 with the 3A cable in the box.

### Will these cables transfer data at 10Gbps from an iPhone 15 Pro?
No. Every cable in this guide is USB 2.0 at 480 Mbps. Apple says iPhone 15 Pro and later Pro models need an optional USB 3 cable for speeds up to 10 Gbit per second.

### Can I use one of these cables to charge my laptop?
Yes, up to 60W with a 3A cable. For a 65W or 100W laptop charger, use a 5A e-marked cable such as the Portronics Konnect B 100W.
