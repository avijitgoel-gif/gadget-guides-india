## The distinctive part: playtime claims depend on volume

### Why the volume matters

A neckband's battery drains faster the louder you play it. So "60 hours" only means something next to a test condition. Most Indian brands do not print one on Amazon. A few do on their own websites, and those are worth reading.

- **boAt Rockerz 255 Pro+:** boAt's product page lists "Playback Time: 60 Hours (at 60% volume)" with a 300 mAh battery. Note that boAt's own support page for the same model still lists 40 hours, and boAt's 2021 launch blog also said 40 hours.
- **Zebronics Zeb Evolve:** zebronics.com lists "Playback time 20h" marked "*at 100% Volume", with Bluetooth v6.0 and country of origin India. Zebronics' shop site, shop.zebronics.com, lists the same Evolve with "Playback time 17H" marked "*at 50% Volume", Bluetooth 5.0 and country of origin China.
- **boAt Rockerz 113, Noise Airwave and Portronics Harmonics Z5:** the brand pages we checked give a playtime figure but no test volume.

### 60% is also the safe listening number

Here is the useful coincidence. The World Health Organization's safe listening advice is to keep your device "to no more than 60% of maximum" and to stay below an 80 dB average. WHO says 80 dB is safe for up to 40 hours a week, while 90 dB cuts that to 4 hours a week.

So a claim measured at 60% volume is roughly the battery life you get if you also follow WHO's advice. If you listen louder on a noisy bus, expect much less. One independent reviewer, Geekman, reported a little over 18 hours from an older batch of the Rockerz 255 Pro+ at 70% volume, when boAt was still advertising 40 hours.

### A quick sanity check with mAh

Where a brand publishes battery size, divide claimed hours by capacity:

- Rockerz 255 Pro+: 60 h from 300 mAh is 20 hours per 100 mAh.
- Rockerz 113: 40 h from 210 mAh is about 19 hours per 100 mAh.
- Harmonics Z5: 33 h from 250 mAh is about 13 hours per 100 mAh.

The two boAt claims are the most aggressive. That does not make them false, but it is a reason to treat them as best case figures at moderate volume. A brand that will not tell you either the mAh or the test volume gives you nothing to check.

## Buying guide: how to choose a neckband under ₹1,000

1. **Ask for the test volume.** Treat any playtime without a volume as a best case. boAt (255 Pro+) and Zebronics publish one; use those numbers to compare.
2. **Check the BIS R-number.** Wireless headphones and earphones have been under BIS compulsory registration since 1 April 2021. The R-number is printed on the box or product; verify it on the BIS registration portal (crsbis.in). BIS is moving these products from IS 616:2017 to IS/IEC 62368-1:2023, with the old standard allowed until 1 November 2028, and the R-number stays the same after migration.
3. **Dual pairing beats a higher Bluetooth number.** Bluetooth 6.0 on a ₹499 neckband does not add codecs or range by itself. Dual pairing changes your day if you use a phone and laptop.
4. **Water rating for workouts.** IPX5 handles sweat and splashes; IPX7 (Rockerz 255 Pro+) is rated for brief immersion. "Not Water Resistant" means keep it out of the gym.
5. **ENC is for the other person.** ENC cleans up your voice on calls. It does not block noise for you; that needs ANC, which costs more.
6. **Mind the warranty.** All five list 1 year. Zebronics specifies carry in to a service centre.

## FAQ

### Which is the best neckband under Rs 1000 in India?
For most buyers, the boAt Rockerz 255 Pro+ at ₹999. On 27 November 2026 it had 4.0 stars from 2,08,385 ratings on Amazon.in, ranked #6 in In-Ear Headphones, and boAt rates it for 60 hours at 60% volume from a 300 mAh battery with IPX7. The best value pick is the boAt Rockerz 113 at ₹799.

### Will I really get 60 hours from a neckband?
Only near the test volume. boAt states 60 hours at 60% volume for the Rockerz 255 Pro+. Listening louder drains the battery faster; one reviewer measured a little over 18 hours at 70% volume on an older batch.

### What volume is safe for neckband earphones?
WHO advises keeping your device at no more than 60% of maximum and an average below 80 dB. At 80 dB WHO says up to 40 hours a week is safe; at 90 dB only 4 hours a week.

### Do Bluetooth neckbands need BIS certification in India?
Yes. Wireless headphones and earphones are under BIS compulsory registration since 1 April 2021. Check the R-number on the box at crsbis.in.

### Is ENC the same as noise cancellation?
No. ENC (environmental noise cancellation) reduces background noise picked up by the microphone during calls. ANC (active noise cancellation) reduces the noise you hear and is rare under ₹1,000.
