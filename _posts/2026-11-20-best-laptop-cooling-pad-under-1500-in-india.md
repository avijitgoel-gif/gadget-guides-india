## Do laptop cooling pads actually work?

Three sets of published tests give a fair picture.

**Notebookcheck** ran Witcher 3 for over an hour on an MSI gaming laptop with and without a cheap Havit pad from Amazon. Average CPU temperature fell from 92°C to 80°C and GPU temperature from 76°C to 70°C. The catch was noise: measured sound went up from 53.9 to 59.2 dB(A), though the pad's larger fans had a lower pitch than the laptop's own fans. Notebookcheck also warned that results vary for laptops with small air intake grilles on the bottom.

**WIRED** found a USB powered dual fan Targus pad made no difference at all on a Razer Blade 16: maximum CPU temperature stayed at 85°C with and without it. A more powerful Razer pad did bring it down to 77°C. On a budget MSI Cyborg 15, the Razer pad cut the maximum CPU temperature by 20°C but raised benchmark performance by only 3%.

**Laptop Mag** tested several pads on three laptops and found an average drop of 13°F (about 7°C), with some pads managing only 1 or 2 degrees. Processing performance improved by less than 2%.

So a cooling pad is mostly about lower temperatures, a cooler keyboard and less thermal stress over a long session. It will not make a slow laptop fast. If you game or render for hours on a laptop with big vents underneath, it is worth ₹500 to ₹800. If you use a thin office laptop for email, a basic [laptop stand]({{ "/best-laptop-stand-under-1000-in-india/" | relative_url }}) that lifts the back and keeps the vents clear may do as much.

## Check your laptop before you buy

Turn your laptop over. If you see a large grille under the fans, a pad can push cool air straight in. If the bottom is mostly solid and air comes in through the keyboard or sides, a pad will help much less. ElectroStingz, testing a pad on an ASUS ROG Strix G16, found the components above solid parts of the base, such as the SSD, barely changed in temperature, while parts under vents dropped 3 to 8°C at full fan speed.

Then do the free fixes first. HP's support guide on overheating says to use the laptop on a hard, level surface, keep about 15 cm of clearance around each vent, and clear dust from the vents with canned air with the laptop off. It warns that a bed, lap or cushion can restrict or block airflow.

## Buying guide: what matters in a cheap cooling pad

### Fan position beats fan count

A pad only helps if its fans blow into your laptop's intake grilles. Two 125 mm fans in the right place beat six small fans in the wrong place. Look at your laptop's base and compare it with the fan layout in the product photos.

### Size and weight limits

Match the pad to your screen size. A 17 inch laptop on a 15.6 inch pad will overhang the fans and the front lip. The NC230 and NC6500D are the 17 inch options here.

### USB power and noise

Use the speed control if your pad has one: Notebookcheck's test shows a pad can add more than 5 dB of noise at full speed.

### Does a laptop cooling pad really reduce temperature?
Usually, yes. Notebookcheck measured a 12°C drop in average CPU temperature while gaming, and Laptop Mag found an average drop of about 7°C across several pads. But WIRED found a basic dual fan pad made no difference on one laptop. Results depend on whether the laptop takes in air from the bottom.

### Does a cooling pad improve laptop performance?
Only slightly. Laptop Mag measured less than 2% better processing performance, and WIRED saw 3% on a budget gaming laptop even when the CPU ran 20°C cooler. The main gains are lower temperatures and a cooler keyboard.

### Is it safe to use a cooling pad with a laptop?
Yes. A cooling pad only blows air at the base of the laptop. Make sure it does not cover any vents, and keep the laptop on a hard surface. HP warns that soft surfaces such as a bed or lap can block airflow and cause overheating.

### Are 6 fan cooling pads better than 2 fan pads?
Not automatically. Six small fans cover more of the base, while two large fans move air more quietly. What matters most is whether the fans line up with your laptop's intake grilles.
